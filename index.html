Our story

1992. The USSR is gone. Its weapons scientists are still here.

What happens to people who know how to build weapons of mass destruction when the state that employed them disappears? ISTC was the answer. This is the route it took.

Base camp
1992

An agreement signed in Moscow

On 27 November 1992 the European Union with Euratom, Japan, Russia and the United States sign the Agreement establishing ISTC.

What changed

Weapons scientists and engineers get a path to peaceful research and development.

Stage 1
1994–95

Neighbours join

Georgia and Armenia join in 1994, Kazakhstan and the Kyrgyz Republic in 1995.

What changed

Institutes across the Caucasus and Central Asia submit projects to ISTC for review and funding.

Stage 2
1997–2003

The circle widens

Norway and the Republic of Korea accede in the late 1990s. Tajikistan joins in March 2003.

What changed

More Parties bring funding, expertise and new research partners.

Turning point
2015

ISTC moves. It doesn't close.

Russia withdraws. On 17 July 2015 the Astana office becomes the head office, and on 9 December the Parties sign the Agreement Continuing ISTC.

What changed

Membership opens to any country. The work can go wherever the Parties direct.

Stage 3
2017

A wider mandate in force

The Continuation Agreement enters into force in December 2017.

What changed

Four goals: stop the spread of WMD, give scientists peaceful work, build a culture of security, and drive growth through innovation.

Stage 4
2024

30 years. One theme.

On 6 June 2024 guests from 25 countries meet in Astana for “Science for a Safer World”. ISTC launches its Strategic Roadmap 2024–2030.

What changed

New focus areas and an open invitation to new countries.

Next climb
2026

Central Asia & Caucasus + Japan

The Call for Joint Research Proposals pairs teams from five countries with Japanese research groups in energy security, biosafety and water security.

What's next

The Governing Board decides in December 2026. Projects start no earlier than January 2027.

Our mission in 13 words

“Creating peaceful multilateral S&T collaboration for a safer, more secure, and sustainable world.

Our vision

“To be the partner of choice in creating, facilitating, and expediting impactful world-class, peaceful multilateral S&T collaboration aimed at making the world safer, more secure, and more sustainable.”

Peaceful

Civilian science only. Knowledge that could build weapons goes into health, energy, water and safety.

Multilateral

Ten Parties decide together. Teams from several countries work side by side on one project.

Safer & sustainable

Results that lower chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear risks and help economies grow.

How decisions are made

Who decides which projects ISTC funds?

Four levels. Each one checks something different — so only strong, peaceful science gets through.

Level 1

10 Parties

Member countries and the EU. Each appoints a representative to the Governing Board.

Sets the direction
Level 2

Governing Board

Meets twice a year. Approves projects, the budget and new members — by consensus. Chair: Dr. Ronald F. Lehman II.

Decides
Level 3

Scientific Advisory Committee

Advises the Board on the scientific merit of each proposal. Chair: Kenya Suyama, Japan Atomic Energy Agency.

Checks the science
Level 4

Secretariat

Runs projects day to day from Astana and four branch offices. Executive Director: Karina Anguelieva.

Questions we hear

Before you start the climb

Is ISTC only for the former Soviet Union?

No. Since the 2015 Agreement, membership is open to any country, and ISTC can work wherever its Parties direct. Recent programmes cover Africa, the Middle East and Southeast Asia.

Who funds ISTC?

ISTC describes itself as funded by the European Union, Japan and the United States governments. Norway also contributes funding and expertise. Partners fund individual projects.

Who owns the results of a project?

In regular projects, intellectual property stays with the scientist. Background IP stays with its owner. In partner projects, the partner, the institute and ISTC sign a three-way agreement on IP.

Can my institute apply right now?

The 2026 Call closed on 20 August 2026 and proposals are under evaluation. The Governing Board decides in December 2026. Watch istc.int for the next call.

Can a private company take part?

Yes — as a Partner that funds research. In the 2026 Call, a company from a Central Asian or Caucasus country can join as a subcontractor with ISTC's written approval.

Will ISTC just equip our new lab?

No. ISTC funds research projects that can include equipment, but not equipment alone.
