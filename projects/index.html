How a project works

The route to the summit takes 8 stages

Dates below are from the 2026 Call for Joint Research Proposals. Other calls follow the same logic.

Base camp

Build your team

A leading institute in an ISTC member country plus foreign collaborators. In the 2026 Call: at least one group from Armenia, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan or Tajikistan and one from Japan.

You leave with

A consortium — and one person who is both Project Manager and principal investigator.

Stage 1

Summary proposal

A short proposal plus concurrence letters from the participating institutes. 2026 Call: 25 April – 30 May.

You leave with

An initial screening result. Announced 7–10 July.

Stage 2

Full proposal

The complete plan plus a Letter of Expression of Interest from the Japanese collaborator. 2026 Call: 10 July – 20 August.

You leave with

Goals, tasks and a budget — up to $250,000 for up to three years.

Stage 3

Peer review

Independent experts review the science. Then the Scientific Advisory Committee gives its evaluation.

You leave with

A shortlist. Shortlisted teams collect Host Government Concurrence letters.

Stage 4

Governing Board decision

The Board approves projects by consensus. For the 2026 Call it meets in December 2026.

You leave with

A written decision. Projects start no earlier than January 2027.

Stage 5

Agreement & Work Plan

ISTC and the institute sign a Project Agreement based on the Model Project Agreement. The Work Plan is its annex — a free WP Validator checks it against ISTC rules.

You leave with

Clear tasks, deliverables and rights, including intellectual property.

Stage 6

The climb

Grants go to team members, ISTC buys the equipment, and the team files quarterly financial reports and timesheets. Travel to collaborators is supported.

You leave with

Results — with ISTC staff monitoring the work at every stage.

Summit

Results that last

New IP is recorded and reported. A Technology Implementation Plan helps the team “graduate” to new funding: industry contacts, market intelligence, patent support and business training.

You leave with

A team that keeps working after the grant ends.

Sources: Call for Joint Research Proposals, FAQ, Project development and execution.

Applicant questions

What teams ask us most

Will ISTC help us find a Japanese partner?

No. To keep the call fair, teams find collaborators themselves — through journals, networks and institute websites.

Can one proposal include several Japanese groups?

Yes. A proposal can include more than one Japanese research group. Partners from Europe or the US can join too; if two proposals are equally strong, the one with a third-country partner gets preference.

Is it only fundamental research?

No. Applied research that moves results toward commercial use is welcome, as long as it addresses energy security, biosafety and biosecurity, or water safety and security.

What does the Japanese side's budget cover?

Collaboration costs, not salaries or core research: travel for joint experiments or field surveys, joint conference presentations and article processing charges for joint papers.

What happens to our existing IP?

Background IP stays with its owner. The Project Agreement sets the rules for new (foreground) IP, including licence rights for the funding party.

Can one person lead two proposals?

No. One person can be Lead PI and Project Manager on one proposal only — and those two roles must be the same person. A scientific leader can share the work.
